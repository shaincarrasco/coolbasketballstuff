About this model

What fit means

Fit is a 0 to 100 score for how well five players are predicted to play together, based on their predicted net rating (points scored minus points allowed per 100 possessions). 50 is an average lineup, 73 is a clearly good one and 88 is elite. All 177,450 possible fives across 30 rosters are scored.

A simple linear model (ridge regression) ranks lineups slightly better than XGBoost on teams neither model saw (0.51 vs 0.50). With a few thousand noisy box-score lineups, most of the signal is simply how good the five players are. The app keeps XGBoost because it can say exactly how much each trait adds or costs, which powers “Why this works.”

Data

Box scores from ESPN's public API for the 2025-26 and 2026-27 seasons: 812 games, the latest on Nov 11, 2026. The model learned from 1,384 starting and closing fives, then scored every possible five on all 30 current rosters.

Estimates and approximations

  • Lineups come from box scores, not play-by-play. Each game adds its starting five, plus its closing five (the five players with the most minutes) when that's different.
  • A lineup's net rating is the average plus-minus of its five players, scaled to 100 possessions. Possessions are estimated from shots, free throws, turnovers and offensive rebounds.
  • Offense and defense are split by assuming the lineup's edge over the team's game result is shared evenly.
  • Player impact numbers are box-score estimates built from the same games, so test results are a little optimistic.
  • Rosters are ESPN's current rosters, up to 17 players by minutes. Injury labels are ESPN's status when the page was built.
  • The matchup adjustment is a hand-set rule, not part of the model: it penalizes non-shooters against strong rim protection and turnover-prone players against ball pressure, and rewards a size edge, capped at 4 points either way.
  • The rotation splits 240 minutes by each player's share of season minutes (at most 36), with starters at 30 to 34 minutes and bench players at 14 to 18. The closing five ignores minutes.
  • Pair scores average every five that includes both players. They show association, not cause.
  • Scores are point predictions with no error bars. Treat close calls as ties.
